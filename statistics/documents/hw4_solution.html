Asymptotic Behavior, Law of Large Numbers, and Central Limit Theorem in System Security

Work Done

In this study, I modeled the cumulative security penetration of n systems subjected to m attackers as a discrete-time stochastic process with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments. I implemented an interactive simulation engine in JavaScript comparing the Bernoulli and Rademacher increment models across three different scaling regimes: Total Score (Sn), Sample Average (Sn / n), and Normalized Score (Sn / √n). This visually and empirically demonstrates the Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and the Central Limit Theorem (CLT).

1. Mathematical Models of Penetration Increments

Let X1, X2, …, Xn be independent and identically distributed random variables representing the penetration outcome at each system layer (from 1 to n) for a given attacker trajectory:

  • Bernoulli Model: Xi ∈ {0, 1} with P(Xi = 1) = p and P(Xi = 0) = 1 − p.
    Here, Xi = 1 indicates a successful breach of system i, while 0 indicates a blocked attempt. The expected value is μ = E[Xi] = p and the variance is σ2 = Var(Xi) = p(1 − p).
  • Rademacher Model: Xi ∈ {−1, +1} with P(Xi = +1) = P(Xi = −1) = 1/2.
    This represents a symmetric, zero-mean random walk where an attacker either gains ground (+1) or is pushed back by defensive countermeasures (−1). Here, the mean is μ = E[Xi] = 0 and the variance is σ2 = Var(Xi) = 1.

3. Asymptotic Behavior and Variance Analysis

Let Sn = ∑i=1n Xi be the aggregate score after n steps, where increments have mean μ and variance σ2. By applying linearity of expectation and the properties of variance for independent variables (Var(aY) = a2Var(Y)), we obtain distinct asymptotic regimes as n → ∞:

Quantity Expected Value E[·] Variance Var(·) Behavior as n → ∞
Total Score: Sn n · μ n · σ2 Variance diverges to ∞. The spread of trajectories widens proportionally to √n.
Average Score: Sn / n μ σ2 / n Variance collapses to 0. Converges in probability to the constant μ (Law of Large Numbers).
Normalized Score: Sn / √n √n · μ (0 if μ = 0) σ2 Variance stabilizes at a constant σ2. For zero-mean processes, converges in distribution to N(0, σ2) (Central Limit Theorem).
