Homework: Stochastic Penetration Process

Asymptotic Behavior, Law of Large Numbers, and Central Limit Theorem in System Security

Work Done

In this study, I modeled the cumulative security penetration of n systems subjected to m attackers as a discrete-time stochastic process with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments. I implemented an interactive simulation engine in JavaScript comparing the Bernoulli and Rademacher increment models across three different scaling regimes: Total Score (Sn), Sample Average (Sn / n), and Normalized Score (Sn / √n). This visually and empirically demonstrates the Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and the Central Limit Theorem (CLT).

1. Mathematical Models of Penetration Increments

Let X1, X2, …, Xn be independent and identically distributed random variables representing the penetration outcome at each system layer (from 1 to n) for a given attacker trajectory:

  • Bernoulli Model: Xi ∈ {0, 1} with P(Xi = 1) = p and P(Xi = 0) = 1 − p.
    Here, Xi = 1 indicates a successful breach of system i, while 0 indicates a blocked attempt. The expected value is μ = E[Xi] = p and the variance is σ2 = Var(Xi) = p(1 − p).
  • Rademacher Model: Xi ∈ {−1, +1} with P(Xi = +1) = P(Xi = −1) = 1/2.
    This represents a symmetric, zero-mean random walk where an attacker either gains ground (+1) or is pushed back by defensive countermeasures (−1). Here, the mean is μ = E[Xi] = 0 and the variance is σ2 = Var(Xi) = 1.

3. Asymptotic Behavior and Variance Analysis

Let Sn = ∑i=1n Xi be the aggregate score after n steps, where increments have mean μ and variance σ2. By applying linearity of expectation and the properties of variance for independent variables (Var(aY) = a2Var(Y)), we obtain distinct asymptotic regimes as n → ∞:

Quantity Expected Value E[·] Variance Var(·) Behavior as n → ∞
Total Score: Sn n · μ n · σ2 Variance diverges to ∞. The spread of trajectories widens proportionally to √n.
Average Score: Sn / n μ σ2 / n Variance collapses to 0. Converges in probability to the constant μ (Law of Large Numbers).
Normalized Score: Sn / √n √n · μ (0 if μ = 0) σ2 Variance stabilizes at a constant σ2. For zero-mean processes, converges in distribution to N(0, σ2) (Central Limit Theorem).

Law of Large Numbers (Convergence in Probability of Sn / n)

When selecting Average Score: S_n / n in the simulator and increasing n to 1000, the variance σ2 / n approaches zero. Visually, all attacker trajectories compress into a tight tube that converges toward the theoretical mean μ (0.5 for standard Bernoulli, 0 for Rademacher), leaving the terminal histogram as a single sharp spike (degenerate distribution).

Central Limit Theorem (Convergence in Distribution of Sn / √n)

Dividing by n crushes the random fluctuations entirely, whereas not dividing at all (Sn) lets the fluctuations grow unboundedly. Scaling by 1 / √n is the exact mathematical threshold that keeps the variance constant at Var(Sn / √n) = (1/n) · nσ2 = σ2.

In the Rademacher case (where μ = 0 and σ2 = 1), the quantity Sn / √n has mean 0 and variance 1 for every n. By the Central Limit Theorem, as n grows large, the discrete binomial-like steps smooth out and converge in distribution to the Standard Normal Distribution N(0, 1), as shown by the orange bell curve matching the empirical histogram in the simulator. (Note: For the Bernoulli model where μ = p > 0, Sn / √n has stable variance p(1−p), but its center drifts rightward at speed √n · p unless centered as (Sn − np) / √n).

4. Interpretation in the Context of n Systems and m Attackers

  • Role of System Size (n): The parameter n represents the depth of the infrastructure (e.g., number of defense layers, microservices, or sequential vulnerabilities). A larger n makes the average penetration rate (Sn / n) highly predictable for defenders due to the Law of Large Numbers: random lucky streaks by an attacker are averaged out across many systems. However, the absolute uncertainty (standard deviation of total compromised assets Sn) still grows at rate √n.
  • Role of the Number of Attackers (m): Each attacker represents an independent realization (sample trajectory) of the stochastic process. If m is very small (e.g., 1 or 2 attackers), the defender only observes isolated random paths and cannot reliably estimate the true vulnerability parameter p. As m increases (a massive botnet or concurrent threat actors), the empirical distribution across attackers accurately reconstructs the theoretical probability distribution. Furthermore, a larger m increases the extreme value risk: with hundreds of attackers operating concurrently, the probability that at least one trajectory reaches the upper tail of the normal distribution (a severe multi-system breach) becomes near-certain.
